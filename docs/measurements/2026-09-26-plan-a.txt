Section: The Debug flag and the log level
The same info device reply read system.debug : 0 and system.log.level : 1, which is none. The updater of a release build sets both so: applications/system/updater/util/update_task_worker_flasher.c sets the log level to none at line 352 and clears the Debug flag at line 353, in the branch for builds without FURI_DEBUG.
The UART capture, docs/measurements/logs/2026-09-25-uart.txt, bears this out. Its last device lines before today are the RAM updater's own boot during the flash of 2026-09-25 at 20:15, up to 20:16:02. Section: The settings after the update
sr status read volume 30 after the flash, 40 before it. /int/.momentum_settings.txt now differs from the copy inside the update's own backup, /ext/update/f7-update-mntm-screen-reader-a0dea084/backup.tar, taken at the update's start, in four keys:
lock_on_boot: true now, false in the backup.
dark_mode: false now, true in the backup.
rpc_color_bg: 4286709760 now, 16777215 in the backup.
sr_volume: 30 now, 40 in the backup.
The other 41 keys match, among them menu_style: 0, the List style, and screen_reader: true.
Every key of the file now holds its compiled default, except sr_volume: 30, where the default is 100. rpc_color_bg shows that the file was written from settings that were never loaded from a file. The load clamps that key to 0xFFFFFF, 16777215, so the default 0xFF820000, 4286709760, cannot come back from a file once it has been through one. So the file was saved during the post-update boot. The updater's flash stage marks /int to be wiped before the restore (line 345 of the same file). So in the post-update boot, a special boot, the settings load at card mount finds no file, and the settings stay at their defaults. The updater then restores /int from the backup and unpacks the resources for minutes, and a save in that time replaces the restored file.
Nothing saves the settings by itself. The saves are the Momentum app's on exit, the desktop lock menu's, and the reader's: its volume chord, Back and Left or Right, a step of 10 each time; its toggle chord; and sr on, sr off, sr volume and sr delay on the console. Seven Back and Left chords would turn the default 100 into exactly 30. The console was gone during the update and no key was sent from this Mac, so keys pressed on the device are the likeliest explanation. With the reader on by default since c00eed5c9, it runs during that boot and speaks the updater's screens at volume 100. The flashes of 2026-09-25 kept the settings; the reader was off by default then.
