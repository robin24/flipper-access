Section: Open point (a), what the device does with a chunk

The firmware (applications/services/storage/storage_cli.c, storage_cli_write_chunk): it opens the file for appending, says Ready, then reads the console's pipe in slices of 1,024 bytes (buffer_size) and writes each slice to the file; at the first slice that cannot be written it prints the error and stops reading. The console's pipe holds 192 bytes (cli_vcp.c: VCP_BUF_SIZE is three USB packets of 64 bytes), so the host's write of a chunk is held back by the USB flow control until the device has taken nearly all of it. The count is read as a 32-bit unsigned number; there is no other limit on a chunk.
The device refused no size that was tried: chunks of 8, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1,024 and 4,096 KB all arrived whole. A count of no bytes: storage write_chunk "/ext/spike/empty.bin" 0 said Ready, and storage stat said File, size: 0b. A count the number cannot hold, or no number, is answered with the usage text and no Ready (its first lines):
storage write_chunk "/ext/spike/refuse.bin" 4294967296
Usage:
storage <cmd> <path> <args>
The path must start with /int or /ext
The same for the counts -1 and abc, and for none.
What reaches the console when the device stops reading early was tried with letters only, no control characters: storage write_chunk "/ext/spike/leftover.bin" 16, Ready, then the 16 bytes and 1,024 or 8,192 letters z after them. The device took the 16 and read the rest as keystrokes: every letter was echoed (1,024 and 8,192 of them), and a line end then ran them as one command:
could not find command `zzzz...zzzz`, try `help`
With 1,024 letters the write returned at once and the answer came 0.014 s after the line end; with 8,192 the write took 0.57 s and the answer 0.10 s. Free heap (sr status) fell from 118,160 to 116,768 with 1,024 letters (1,392 bytes) and to 107,168 with 8,192 (10,992 bytes, about 1.3 bytes for each letter); it was back at 118,240 after the next commands (a second run gave the same to within 32 bytes). The console's line has no length limit (cli_shell_line.c pushes every key onto a string). If the cost keeps to 1.3 bytes a letter, the bytes left behind by a chunk of 32 KB would take about 44 KB of the 118 KB of free heap while the console read them, and a chunk of 64 KB about 88 KB. Neither was tried, because a failed allocation there would most likely crash the device; the test with 8,192 letters is the largest made. last crash in sr status was the same line afterwards.
So after a write that fails halfway through a chunk, the bytes left over are not harmless noise, and what they cost grows with the chunk. The install script stops at the first StorageError, but a console with a long line in it, or a device short of memory, is what it leaves behind.

Section: Seconds over USB set against the goal of 15 minutes

The voice by the pack: 653.3 s to send it and 963.7 s to unpack it onto a card without the set = 1,617 s, 26 minutes 57 seconds; over a set already there 653.3 + 786.3 = 1,439.6 s, 24 minutes.
The voice by files: 1,581.3 s, 26 minutes 21 seconds.
So the two ways are alike on this card, and the pack is no faster. The console is not what limits them: the bytes go over it at 175 to 200 KB a second in a single file, whatever the chunk size, while the extract moves nothing over the console and writes at 118 to 145 KB a second, so the limit is the card's writes through the storage service. The pack writes the bytes twice (the tar, then the clips) and the files write them once; what the pack saves is the 36 ms stat and the console's round trips for each of the 10,537 files, and what it costs is the second write.
The update package adds 71.6 s. Not measured: info device, the backup (update backup), and the updater's two restarts.
The install over USB with the voice therefore takes about 25 to 28 minutes for the transfers alone, whichever way, and exceeds 15 minutes. The card reader stays the default answer (Robin, 2026-09-28). The figures point at the bytes, not the protocol: the voice is 103.6 MB of clips (108,586,532 bytes), which alone take 9 to 10 minutes at the card's 175 to 200 KB a second.

Section: The constants

DEFAULT_CHUNK = 32768: from 32 KB the speed is flat (28.45 s against 28.06 s for 64 KB over eight runs each, the runs of one size spreading over 4 s), nothing above 32 KB is faster, no size failed, and the smaller of the fast sizes leaves the fewest bytes behind in the console when a write fails (open point a). 8 KB took 25 percent longer on the file (35.68 against 28.45 s) and 12 percent on the folder (88.43 against 78.92 s).
EXTRACT_SECONDS_PER_MB = 13: the slower extract, onto a card with nothing of the set, took 8.66 s for each megabyte; with half again on top that is 12.99, so 13. For the heart pack (111.3 MB) the mute would be 1,447 s, 24 minutes, against the extract's 964. A pack with the same number of entries and far fewer bytes costs more for each megabyte: taking the 5 MB file's 5.7 s a megabyte for the bytes, the 111.3 MB would take about 634 s, which leaves about 330 s, 31 ms an entry, for making 10,604 entries. The three sets made so far are all 100 to 110 MB with 10,535 clips.
