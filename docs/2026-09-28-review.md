# Review of Sonar and Flipper Access, 28 September 2026

Robin asked for a thorough review of the firmware, the tools and the release process before the first release, with the release process's holes in particular, since it has never run. This document holds the findings and the handoff for the next session. It is written for a screen reader: prose and lists, no tables.

The review was done by Claude, as one coordinating session and five parallel reviewers: the speech engine; the input chords, console and settings; the GUI tap and the screen model; the tools and tests; and the release process. Every finding below was checked against the source by the coordinating session before it went in; findings that needed more than reading say how they were checked.

## How to read this

- Severity: High means a user will lose something or the release will not work as designed; Medium means wrong behaviour a user will meet, or a gap that will cost a release; Low means a corner case, a small inefficiency, or an improvement.
- "Verified" means checked in the code, with a host harness, or in the CI's records. Nothing was tried on a device; the findings that need the device are collected in "Checks on the device".
- Each finding has an identifier: F for the reader service, the input chords and the settings, S for the speech engine, G for screens the reader gets wrong, R for the release process, T for the tools and tests.
- Line numbers are those of the reviewed commits.

## What was reviewed

- The firmware fork at 86820a7, the submodule's pinned commit, against its Momentum base d3f89dfe2 (upstream dev of 18 August 2026): 90 commits, 80 files, about 5,850 added lines. Every file of applications/services/screen_reader was read, together with the fork's changes to the GUI, input, desktop, dolphin, storage-facing code, settings, the crash handler, FreeRTOS's configuration and the build system.
- This repository at cf51c96: every tool, the host tests and tests/fbt, the CI workflow, the specs, the plans for publishing and the README.
- The CI's history through the GitHub API: the Build workflow has 17 runs on record, numbered up to 24, the recent ones green in about 8.5 minutes, the latest reporting "firmware.bin 866296 bytes, limit 880640, spare 14344 bytes". Neither repository has a tag, flipper-access has no release, and the fork has no workflow runs.
- The host tests were run: all pass (the counts are under "Checked and found sound").

## The most important findings

1. F2: without a working SD card, the reader's volume and toggle chords save the compiled defaults over every setting in /int/.momentum_settings.txt, and a silent reader invites exactly those chords.
2. S1: during NFC-V emulation, speech can take the speaker between two frames and freeze the emulation until the utterance ends.
3. F1: speech uses DMA2 channel 4, which the external CC1101 module's transmitter also uses, so speech during a transmission corrupts it.
4. G1 to G3: the byte input (NFC keys, LFRFID data, Bad USB IDs) is unreadable, keyboard screens open without their prompt or value, and the number and text inputs announce characters that were never typed.
5. R1 and R4: the voice pack cannot be unpacked on the device, and the install plan's order does not work; the console's existing `storage extract` replaces the planned unpack command once the pack format is fixed.
6. R2 and R3: a release build can report itself as mntm-dev, or as rc1, and card_prune then deletes the release the user has just installed.
7. T1 and F3: a Ctrl-C during a voice sync can hang the tool and leave the device muted until a reboot, and a scripted run cut off mid-chord leaves the chords stuck.
8. R5 and R6: nothing defines how the Mac-built voice packs get into a tag-triggered release, and no build keeps its firmware.elf, map or SDK, so crash addresses from a release could not be decoded.
9. The README's voice section still describes /ext/sr/voice and Piper: a user who follows it gets a silent reader.
10. R10: the release needs a complete source offer (the submodules and the apps' sources) and proper licence texts in the voice packs.

## Firmware: the service, the input chords, the settings

### F1. Speech and the external CC1101 radio share DMA2 channel 4 (High, verified in the code)

- Where: applications/services/screen_reader/speech_hw.c lines 9 to 10 (DMA2, channel 4), 69 (the interrupt) and 77 to 82 (the stop); applications/drivers/subghz/cc1101_ext/cc1101_ext.c lines 28 to 36 (DMA2 channels 3, 4 and 5), 881 to 892 and 947.
- What happens: the external module's asynchronous transmit uses channel 4 to toggle its data pin through the GPIO port's BSRR register on every TIM17 update. Speech programs the same channel for the speaker. Speech during a transmission reprograms channel 4, so the transmitted data stops toggling and the module sends a corrupted signal, or a steady carrier; when speech ends, speech_hw_stop disables channel 4 and ends what was left of the transmission. The other order is worse: a transmission that starts while speech plays reconfigures the channel while speech's half- and full-transfer interrupts are still enabled, so the speech interrupt fires at the transmitter's rate. The speaker mutex guards TIM16, but nothing guards the DMA channel.
- How likely: the reader announces the screen change a transmission brings, so a blind user who transmits with an external module is likely to hit it. The internal CC1101 uses DMA2 channels 1 and 2 and is not affected.
- The channels in use: on DMA2, 1 and 2 (internal CC1101, infrared, RFID; 2 also the NFC-V signal reader), 3 and 5 (the NFC-V signal reader, the external CC1101), 6 and 7 (the SD card's SPI). On DMA1, 1 and 2 (digital_sequence, which the NFC listeners transmit with), 4 and 5 (pulse_reader, which nothing in the tree allocates), 6 and 7 (serial receive). DMA1 channel 3 has no user in the tree.
- Fix: move speech to DMA1 channel 3 (its interrupt Dma1Ch3, its HT3 and TC3 flags, the same DMAMUX request TIM16_UP), after checking once more that nothing built in uses it. Confirm on the device.

### F2. Without a working SD card, the reader's chords overwrite all saved settings with the defaults (High, verified in the code)

- Where: furi/flipper.c lines 186 to 213 load the settings at boot only when the SD card is ready, and otherwise only at a later card mount (flipper_mount_callback, lines 131 to 156), although the file is on internal storage (lib/momentum/settings.h line 8, INT_PATH). The fork's saves: screen_reader.c lines 555 (the volume chords), 608 (the toggle chord) and 652 (sr on and off), and screen_reader_cli.c lines 213, 391 and 409.
- What happens: with the card missing, unformatted or failing, the settings in RAM are the compiled defaults. The reader is silent without a card, and the natural reaction, Back and Right for more volume or Back and Down held to turn the reader on, saves the defaults over every setting in /int/.momentum_settings.txt: the menu style, the lock settings, dark mode, the reader's volume and voice set, everything. When a card appears, the overwritten file is what gets loaded. The loss is silent and permanent.
- Upstream exposure is small: there, only the lock menu's switches can save without a card, since the Momentum app is itself on the card.
- Fix: load the settings in the "SD card not ready" branch too, since the file does not depend on the card; or keep a "loaded" flag in settings.c and refuse to save until a load has run.

### F3. A console "input send" without its release leaves the chords stuck (Medium, verified with a harness)

- Where: sr_chords.c with applications/services/input/input_cli.c lines 47 to 62: the console's input command goes through the filter.
- What happens: after `input send back press` with no release, the chord machine believes Back is held. Every physical press of Up, OK, Left or Right is then swallowed as a chord and runs its command (Back and Left even lowers and saves the volume) until Back is physically pressed and released. With the hardware Back held, `input send ok press` makes the next physical OK press disappear.
- Why it matters: tools/sr_drive.py sends exactly these raw back press and back release commands, so a scripted run that stops mid-chord (a serial time-out, a Ctrl-C) leaves the device this way, and to a blind user it sounds like keys reading the screen or changing the volume at random.
- Fix: keep separate chord state for events that come from software, keyed on the event's sequence source (fake_input in input_cli.c leaves sequence_source and sequence_counter uninitialised, an upstream bug, so set them there first).
- For balance: the reviewer's harness also ran about 6.2 million interleavings of the device's own key events (Back once or twice, one or two other keys, up to four reader toggles, the reader starting on or off). Every invariant held: each delivered Press gets its Release, no Short, Long or Repeat arrives without its Press, the machine is idle once all keys are up, no key is swallowed afterwards, Back's Long arrives exactly once when no chord ran, Short and Long are never both delivered, and the extra Long always precedes the Release.

### F4. Each `sr` console command runs on a new 4 KB stack (Low-medium, verified)

- screen_reader_cli.c line 457 registers with cli_registry_add_command, which gives every run of the command a thread with CLI_BUILTIN_COMMAND_STACK_SIZE, 4 KB (lib/toolbox/cli/cli_registry_i.h line 16). `sr watch` holds that stack and about 1.5 KB of queue and buffers for the whole session, beside whatever app runs, and running out of heap crashes the device.
- Fix: cli_registry_add_command_ex with about 2 KB; the deepest `sr` path, the status command or a settings save, needs about 1 KB.

### F5. The volume chord writes the settings file before it speaks (Low-medium)

- screen_reader.c lines 554 to 558: every tap of Back and Left or Right rewrites the whole settings file (about 45 keys) on the reader's thread before "Volume 60" is queued, so the feedback lags, and five taps are five full rewrites of internal flash. The toggle (lines 607 to 608) does the same.
- Fix: speak first, then save; or save once, about two seconds after the last change.

### F6. Frames dropped at the hand-over are never recovered (Low)

- sr_frame_end (screen_reader.c lines 147 to 166) waits two ticks for the mutex and otherwise drops the frame without asking for another. If that was the last frame of a screen that then stays still, the reader never announces it. It is rare, since the service holds the mutex for well under a millisecond and priority inheritance applies.
- Fix: on a drop, set a flag that makes the service call gui_update once it has finished.

### F7. The crash address is only useful for failed checks (Low, improvement)

- check.c stores the return address of the call into the crash routine. For HardFault, NULL pointer dereference, UsageFault, BusFault and stack overflow (furi_hal_interrupt.c lines 294 to 363, furi_hal_os.c line 209), that call is made by the fault handler, so the address points into the handler. The README says so.
- Improvement: in the fault handlers, take the program counter from the exception frame the core pushed and store that instead; for a stack overflow, store part of the thread's name in the second register. The idle crash hunt would then get an address for every kind of crash.

### F8. Chord corner cases (Low, verified with the harness)

- A chord key held across two holds of Back charges its command to the second hold: Back down, OK down, Back up, Back down again, OK held long runs Spell and drops the second Back's own Short or Long.
- With the reader off: Back held long, Down pressed, Back released first, Down held long delivers Back's Long (on the lock screen that opens the power-off dialog) and also turns the reader on.
- By design but worth knowing: Back's Repeats are dropped and its Long waits for the release even with the reader off, so hold-Back-to-delete in the text input (text_input.c lines 602 to 622) and hold-to-clear in the byte input (byte_input.c line 718) no longer repeat, for sighted users too, and the filter cannot be switched off.

### F9. Voice set names: the console, the app and the engine disagree (Low)

- `sr voice use` accepts only lower-case letters, digits, hyphens and underscores (speech_voice_set_valid), while the engine, `sr voice list` and the Momentum app's Voice row accept any folder name that is not hidden and has no slash, so a set the app lists can be refused by the console. The Voice row cannot go back to "auto" once a set has been picked.

### F10. Smaller notes (Low)

- The missing-word memory is a ring of 64 hashes (speech_voice.h line 23), so "each at most once per boot" (README lines 310 and 467) holds only up to 64 words; after that, words come back into missing.txt.
- RTC backup registers 8 and 9, the fork's crash address and build, are not used by the official firmware or upstream Momentum; another fork using them would only give a wrong "last crash" line.

## The speech engine

### S1. During NFC-V emulation, speech can take the speaker between two frames and freeze the emulation (High, verified in the code)

- Where: lib/signal_reader/signal_reader.c lines 190 to 222 (the fork's first-start delay and speaker acquire) and 330 to 345 (the release at stop); targets/f7/furi_hal/furi_hal_nfc_iso15693.c lines 362 to 387, which start and stop the parser, and with it the signal reader, around every wait for a frame; lib/signal_reader/parsers/iso15693/iso15693_parser.c lines 263 to 283, which restart it after every failed parse; speech.c lines 204 to 229, where speech polls TIM16's bus every 5 ms for up to 500 ms and then takes the speaker.
- What happens: the signal reader releases the speaker mutex after every frame and takes it again, waiting forever, before the next one. Speech that is waiting for TIM16 can take the speaker in that gap; the NFC thread then waits inside signal_reader_start until the utterance ends and the 200 ms idle hold passes, the emulated tag stops answering, and the reader's transaction fails. A realistic trigger is built in: the ISO15693 emulate screen appends "R: ..." to its text box on every custom command (applications/main/nfc/helpers/protocol_support/iso15693_3/iso15693_3.c lines 85 to 97), and the reader announces that change, as long hex speech, in the middle of the exchange. This contradicts the README's "the emulation takes over the speaker, and the reader stays silent until you leave".
- Also: the 400 ms first-start delay applies to every emulation start, with the reader off too.
- Fix: hold the speaker for the listener's whole life, taking it at the first start and releasing it when the listener ends, both on the NFC thread; per frame, only stop the counter, or reset TIM16 while keeping the mutex. Skip the 400 ms delay when the reader is off.

### S2. Long expansions are cut in the middle of a word, and the fragment becomes a clip (Medium, verified with a fuzz harness)

- Where: speech_text.c lines 65 to 69 (out_char drops characters once the 800-byte buffer is full), used from speech.c line 555. speech_text.h line 14 assumes about 4.7 times expansion; numbers of three and four digits expand 7 to 10 times.
- What happens: of 300,000 fuzzed inputs, 28,192 expansions were cut and 17,840 of those ended in a partial word. "7777 " thirty-one times (155 characters) ends in "seven se", with about 18 of the 31 numbers spoken; 159 digits with a leading zero end in "fiv". The fragment fails its clip lookup (a whole-folder scan), is spelled, is logged to missing.txt, and `--pull-missing` then makes it a real clip, since load_list accepts any 1 to 16 lower-case letters: clips such as "se", "fiv" and "ni" end up on the card. The same happens where set_text cuts `sr say` text at 159 characters, where part_length has no space to cut at, and where the screen model's sr_copy and sr_append cut rows at 47, 63 and 159 characters.
- Fix: when the expansion would overflow, go back to the last space (and optionally add "and more"); mark cut items so the worker does not log their last word as missing; cut set_text at a word boundary.

### S3. Every clip open allocates about 0.7 KB of heap for a moment (Medium-low, verified in the configuration)

- Where: targets/f7/fatfs/ffconf.h line 215 sets _FS_TINY to 0, so every open allocates a file object of about 560 bytes with its own sector buffer (storage_ext.c line 420), plus the path copies and the storage node on the storage thread and about 100 bytes on the worker's side.
- What happens: the reader does this for every word and every spelled letter, inside apps that never touch storage themselves. An app sitting near the heap floor can now crash with "out of memory" in the storage thread while the reader speaks.
- Fix: before opening clips, check memmgr_heap_get_max_free_block against about 4 KB, and below it complete the item muted and count it. Measure with ram_probe.

### S4. `sr voice off` does not stop the item being spoken (Low-medium, a known limit in the roadmap)

- speech.c line 541 reads voice_enabled only when an item starts, so the item already playing keeps opening clips and writes missing.txt after the console command has returned, contrary to speech.h lines 82 to 84 ("no file is opened"). missing.txt can reappear right after `--pull-missing` removed it, and if the worker reaches a clip that is being uploaded, storage_file_open waits for the other handle while the DMA keeps running (see S6).
- Fix: speech_set_voice_clips(false) also calls speech_stop, and the console command waits, bounded, until the engine is idle before it replies.

### S5. A card or set folder that goes away mid-item gives seconds of silence and false missing words (Low-medium, verified in the code)

- Where: speech.c lines 466 to 478 and 501 to 509.
- What happens: after the card is removed, or the set's folder is deleted or renamed while the set is cached, every word fails and is spelled, and speech_spell_word pushes a 60 ms gap before every letter although every letter clip fails too: a 159-character item becomes about 6 to 8 seconds of silence with the speaker held and the queue blocked. Up to eight real words and letter names ("s", "ay") are logged as missing and written to the set's missing.txt when the card comes back. A card swapped between two items is not noticed at all, since storage_sd_status is fine again and nothing listens for the mount (speech.c lines 361 to 362 say otherwise).
- Fix: push the letter gap only after a letter clip opened; end the item and forget the set when an open fails with "not ready" or an internal error, or twice in a row; let the reader's existing card mount callback invalidate the cached set.

### S6. An underrun replays old audio, and a blocked worker loops the ring (Low)

- Where: speech_hw.c line 60 (circular DMA) and speech.c lines 141 to 167.
- What happens: a half that has been played is never cleared, so a late worker (a slow open, a busy storage thread, a file already open) lets the DMA replay the sound of 164 ms before, and a blocked worker loops the ring until it is free again.
- Fix: in the interrupt, fill the half that just finished with silence before setting its flag, about 20 µs every 82 ms.

### S7. Bursts of wake-ups can end a DMA wait early (Low, an upstream bug)

- furi/core/thread.c lines 609 to 616 reduce the remaining time-out by the whole time since the start at every wake-up, so several wake-ups during one wait make it expire early; speech.c line 128 treats that as the end of the utterance. Four non-interrupting pushes 45 ms into a wait end it at 65 ms, with the half due at 78 ms, and the utterance is cut mid-word.
- Fix: in speech_wait_half_played, continue on an error and let the loop's own "waited" check decide; the upstream function could keep its own deadline.

### S8. The speaker stays held while keys keep coming (Low)

- speech.c lines 624 to 632: the 200 ms idle release restarts on any flag, and every key press calls speech_stop, even with the reader off (screen_reader.c lines 171 to 181). Quick navigation after speech keeps the speaker, so beeps are skipped and an NFC thread waits (S1).
- Fix: measure the idle time from the end of the last item.

### S9. The missing-word log (Low)

- speech.c lines 518 to 535 ignore the write results and clear the log anyway, so words are lost on a full card; each word costs two storage calls; and when the file cannot be opened, the log stays full and every item tries again.
- Fix: build the lines in the idle 512-byte file buffer, write once, and clear the log only when the write succeeded.

### S10. Smaller ones (Low)

- An interrupting push sets only the work flag, not the stop flag (speech.c line 703), so the old utterance plays up to about 82 ms more; set the stop flag too when interrupt is true.
- An `sr play` path longer than 159 characters is cut silently: the console prints "playing", then the worker logs "Cannot open".

## Screens the reader gets wrong

The GUI reviewer fed the firmware's own tap code (canvas.c) and the unchanged screen_model.c with draw sequences that follow each module's draw callback, in a host harness. Findings marked "verified" were reproduced there, and the coordinating session checked their causes in the source.

### G1. The byte input screen is misread, and its keys have no names (High, verified)

- Where: applications/services/gui/modules/byte_input.c, the whole draw code (lines 142 to 330 and 590 to 662). It sets no hint at all; the digits and keys are drawn glyph by glyph.
- What happens: with the value 12 34 56 78 and the high digit of the second byte being edited, the tap records "124", "3" (inverted) and "5678": the low digit of the edited byte joins the byte before it. Opening the screen says only "3, 3", the digit being edited and the selected key, both taken as the focus because they are inverted. The prompt and the value are never read. Save and Backspace are icons without a note, so moving onto them says nothing useful.
- Who meets it: NFC key entry (MIFARE Classic keys, Ultralight, DES), LFRFID password and manual data entry, Bad USB VID, PID and MAC.
- Fix: a focus hint on the selected key and notes for Save and Backspace, as text_input.c does; a note with the formatted value, such as "12 34 56 78, byte 2". With any focus hint on the screen, the inverted digit stops counting as focus.

### G2. Keyboard screens open without their prompt or their current value (High, verified)

- Where: screen_model.c lines 170 to 171 (a title must be in the primary font) and 355 to 375 (sr_screen_announcement reads the title and then only the focus when there is one). The prompts of text_input.c (line 294), byte_input.c (line 612) and number_input.c (line 245) are drawn in the secondary font.
- What happens: a text input that opens with the prompt "Name the signal" and the default name "RAW_433" is announced only as "Save". A number input opens as just "9".
- Fix: when a screen has no primary-font title, take the topmost normal row near the top as the title, and read the input field's row with the focus; or give the keyboards a title hint.

### G3. False "typed" announcements on the keyboards (High, verified)

- Where: screen_model.c lines 377 to 406, sr_typed_character, together with the glyph runs of the keyboard rows.
- What happens: in the number input, moving from 9 onto the sign or Save key is announced, interrupting, as the typed character "9": the key row "5678" becomes "56789" once 9 is no longer inverted, which looks exactly like one character added. The text input gives the same false "typed" when Up enters cursor mode.
- Fix: leave keyboard-font rows out of sr_typed_character. That removes the whole class in every keyboard.

### G4. Text input: the cursor mode is silent, and a scrolled or emptied field is misread (Medium, verified)

- Moving the cursor with Left and Right says nothing.
- Once the text is longer than the field, text_input.c (lines 313 to 332) draws "..." and the tail. Typing w is then announced as a change with the whole tail, not as "w".
- Deleting the last character is announced as a new screen, "Backspace", because an empty field redraws the keyboard in upper case (line 338) and most rows then differ. The first character typed into an empty field is a new screen for the same reason.
- Fix: canvas_tap_hint_full_text with the text buffer before the field is drawn; a focus note in cursor mode such as "cursor after r, 3 of 4"; keyboard-font rows left out of the kept and total counts of sr_screen_changed, or compared ignoring case.

### G5. Every text box line ends with a question mark (Medium, verified)

- Where: gui/elements.c lines 815 to 816 count the newline and the final terminator in each line's length, lines 931 to 983 draw every counted character as a glyph, and canvas_tap_glyph (canvas.c line 142) turns every control character into "?".
- What happens: a widget text box reads "Card saved?. to Favorites?". The speech text treats each "?" as the end of a question, so every line ends with a sentence pause, and the console mirror shows the question marks. 22 files use widget text boxes; Sub-GHz Read RAW uses elements_text_box.
- Fix: in canvas_tap_glyph, drop characters below 0x20 instead of mapping them.

### G6. Glyph runs never get spaces (Medium, verified)

- Where: canvas.c lines 144 to 157: any glyph to the right on the same baseline continues the run.
- What happens: keyboard rows become tokens such as "qwertyuiop", "01234567" and "89ABCDEF", and byte values become one long number. Back and Up reads them as nonsense words, and each one is looked up as a clip and then spelled.
- Fix: start a new word when a glyph starts more than one pixel after the previous glyph's right edge (u8g2's glyph width); or leave keyboard-font normal rows out of the description and the announcements.

### G7. Long announcements lose their buttons (Medium, verified)

- Where: screen_model.c line 373 appends the buttons last, and announcements are cut at SR_ANN_TEXT_MAX, 160.
- What happens: a dialog with four lines of text is announced as "... are lost too.. buttons: l". Back and Up still reads everything.
- Fix: build the buttons part first and keep room for it.

### G8. Menu styles other than List (Medium, verified for Vertical)

- Where: menu.c hints the focus only in the List style (line 132). screen_model.c lines 58 to 63 take any inverted secondary-font text at y 61 or lower without a hint for a button.
- What happens: in the Vertical style the lower half of the list is at y 76 to 124, so moving onto GPIO, iButton and the other lower rows is silent. By reading the code, PS4 always reports its "Start" label as the focus, DSi reads "S TAR T", MNTM joins the clock to a list row and rereads the screen at every move, and C64 keeps announcing "N BASIC BYTES FREE" as the heap changes, redrawn every 333 ms.
- List is the default and the README says the other styles are read only where possible, so this is medium. Fix: one canvas_tap_hint_focus(position + 1, count) before each style's selected name.

### G9. Smaller ones (Low)

- The Archive context menu keeps the list's focus hint while it is open (archive_browser_view.c lines 311 to 313), so a move can say "Rename, file.sub", and at verbosity 2 the file's position. Skip the list hint while the menu is open.
- button_menu.c line 54 gives the focus an index and count of 0 although both are known.
- The file browser's position counts the parent folder entry, and the folder's own name is never spoken.
- Icon-only screens outside the desktop, such as the Loading view, are silent (screen_model.c lines 430 to 436).
- elements_button_up and elements_button_down (elements.c lines 219 to 281) have no button hint. The firmware does not use them, but in an outside app their inverted labels would become the focus.
- elements_multiline_text_aligned hyphenates inside words and text_box wraps at any character, so split words are read as two.

## Release process and artifacts

The release has never been run, and plan C (the install script and the release workflow) is not written yet. The continuous build itself is healthy. Everything below is about the step from that build to something a blind user can install, and most of it is cheaper to design into plan C than to retrofit.

### R1. The voice pack cannot be unpacked on the device (High, verified)

- Where: tools/voice_pack.py lines 74 to 88; the firmware's lib/toolbox/tar/tar_archive.c lines 334 to 348 and 409; tests/fbt/test_voice_pack.py lines 62 to 65.
- What happens: the pack holds no folder entries, only files. The firmware's unpacker, which both `storage extract` and the planned install use, opens each file with "create always" and never creates the folders above it, so unpacking into /ext fails at the very first file, sr/voices/<set>/voice.txt, after ten retries 25 ms apart.
- Also: Python's default tar format is PAX, and tar.add passes each clip's fractional modification time, so every clip is preceded by an extra "PaxHeader" entry: about 1 KB more per clip, about 10 MB on a pack of 10,800 clips, each skipped by the device with a warning. The maker's user name and the file times go into the pack too, so two packs of the same set are never byte-identical.
- The test asserts the exact name list without folders, so it protects the defect.
- Fix: open the tar with format=tarfile.USTAR_FORMAT, as the firmware's own scripts/flipper/assets/tarball.py does; add entries for sr/, sr/voices/, sr/voices/<set>/ and each of the 64 bucket folders before the files; set owner, group, user names, mode and time to fixed values in a filter (the time from SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH); update the test to expect the folders; and try one unpack on the device with `storage extract` before the release.

### R2. A tag build can call itself mntm-dev, and card_prune then deletes the release that was just installed (High, verified with local test tags)

- Where: spec section 7; .github/workflows/build.yml line 28 (the depth-1 submodule); Momentum-Firmware/scripts/version.py lines 25 to 44; fbt_options.py line 22; tools/card_prune.py lines 37 to 52.
- What happens: the release reviewer re-created the CI checkout with local test tags. A depth-1 submodule checkout carries the fork's tag only while the tagged commit is still the tip of screen-reader. One commit later, `git describe` finds no tag, version.py writes "mntm-dev" into the firmware, and DIST_SUFFIX from the environment still names the package f7-update-sonar-X.Y.Z. The device then reports mntm-dev, and card_prune, which keeps the running build's folder only by its version or by a "-<hash>" ending, removes f7-update-sonar-X.Y.Z, the release the user has just installed, with its backup.tar.
- Fix: in the release job, fetch the tag explicitly (`git -C Momentum-Firmware fetch --depth 1 origin "refs/tags/$TAG:refs/tags/$TAG"`), check that HEAD is the tag's commit, and after the build check that the built version.json says firmware_version is $TAG. With the explicit fetch, version.py gave "sonar-0.1.0", not dirty, dated by the commit.

### R3. With rc1 and the final tag on one commit, the final build can call itself rc1 (High, verified with local test tags)

- `git describe --tags --exact-match` prefers an annotated tag to a lightweight one, then goes by date or by name. If the rehearsal passes and sonar-0.1.0 is tagged on the same commit as sonar-0.1.0-rc1, which is the expected path, the final firmware can report rc1, and card_prune then keeps the rc1 folder and removes the real one.
- Fix: in the release job, delete every tag but $TAG from the submodule clone before building and check that describe returns $TAG; make all release tags annotated; protect sonar-* tags on both repositories.

### R4. The install plan's order does not work, and the new unpack command is not needed (High, verified)

- Spec section 6 puts the voice on the card in step 3 and installs Sonar in step 5, but its one-archive lever, `sr voice unpack`, would be a Sonar command, which official 1.4.3 and Momentum do not have.
- The console already has `storage extract <archive> <destination>` (applications/services/storage/storage_cli.c lines 524 to 556). It came from upstream in June 2024, so official 1.4.3, Momentum and Sonar all have it, and it calls the same tar_archive_unpack_to, so R1's fix is still needed.
- Fix: drop `sr voice unpack` from the spec; upload the pack, run `storage extract` into /ext, remove the tar; measure the time in the spike. Until the voice is on the card the reader is silent, so install.py should keep narrating on the computer throughout.

### R5. Nothing gets the Mac-built voice packs into a tag-triggered release (High)

- The release runner cannot read files on the Mac, and release.json must list the SHA-256 of every asset, packs included, so it can only be written after the packs are uploaded.
- Fix: release.yml creates a draft release (a prerelease for -rc tags) with the assets CI built; a documented Mac step uploads the two packs with `gh release upload`; a finalising step, a workflow_dispatch job or a local script, writes SHA256SUMS and release.json from the draft's assets; the draft's own assets are installed on the device, then the release is published.

### R6. No firmware.elf, map or SDK is kept for any build (High for crash reports)

- The CI artifact holds only the update .tgz, and it expires after 90 days. The SDK zip is built (dist/f7-C/flipper-z-f7-sdk-...) and thrown away.
- The crash popup's address can only be decoded with the elf of the very build that crashed. The device notes already record that CI images differ from local ones by a few bytes, so a release's elf must come from the release build itself.
- Fix: upload firmware.elf, the map and the SDK zip with every CI run, and attach them to every release.

### R7. The released binary is never the binary that was tested (Medium)

- Version strings are compiled into the image, so the final sonar-0.1.0 image differs from the rc1 image and from any local build, even when the code is identical.
- Fix: install the draft release's own assets on the device and run sr_drive and ram_probe before publishing. Optionally compare the firmware.bin SHA-256 of a local build at the tag with the CI build's.

### R8. The install script has nothing to check the installed firmware against (Medium, verified)

- update.fuf carries only "Info" set to DIST_SUFFIX (scripts/update.py line 197), not the commit.
- Fix: release.json carries the firmware commit, firmware.version and the API version, and install.py compares them, and "dirty: false", with `info device` after the update.

### R9. A release build's About screen sends users to Momentum's website (Medium, verified)

- Where: applications/settings/about/about.c lines 188 to 194. For any build whose version is not mntm-dev, the last line is "momentum-fw.dev".
- Fix: a one-line change there (a small merge surface), or at least a note in the README.

### R10. Licensing gaps (Medium-high)

- GPL source for the binaries: GitHub's automatic source archives of a flipper-access release leave out submodules, and the resources include .fap apps built from Next-Flip/Momentum-Apps at 55a446b, which is not forked. Attach a full source bundle (the fork at the tag with every submodule, archived recursively) or fork Momentum-Apps, name the fork tag and the submodule commits in the release notes, and keep the source available for as long as the binaries are offered.
- The Kokoro pack's LICENSE.txt (voice_pack.py, LICENCES) is one sentence and a link: no Apache-2.0 text, no model version or SHA-256, no dataset attribution, which spec section 9 asks for. The eSpeak pack's text says eSpeak-NG is GPL-3 and the fork's additions CC0 but not which terms the clips themselves carry; pick one.
- A --personal pack gets the release's naming, voice-samantha-sonar-X.Y.Z.tar, lands in the project root, and .gitignore does not cover voice-*.tar. Ignore voice-*.tar, name personal packs differently, and have the release step upload explicit file names and check that each pack's backend is kokoro or espeak.

### R11. Install time: the voice pack is about 100 MB (Medium, estimate)

- The release set has about 10,800 clips. The 2,900-word set was 27 MB, about 9.3 KB a clip, so the release pack is about 100 MB. The console moved 2,828 clips in 442 seconds on 2026-09-24, about 61 KB a second, which puts the pack alone at about 28 minutes against the spec's goal of 15 for everything. One archive instead of 10,800 files removes the per-file overhead but not the volume.
- Options: a card-reader path in install.py, which copies the unpacked pack to the mounted card in about a minute; smaller clips (silence trimmed, a lower sample rate if a listening test through the piezo allows it, or fewer words in the default pack, with the rest arriving through missing.txt); larger write chunks, which the spec already plans to measure.

### R12. Gaps in the continuous build (Medium, verified)

- Momentum's workflow runs `git diff --exit-code` after building; ours does not, and fbt's SDK step rewrites targets/f7/api_symbols.csv when the API drifts, so a drift would pass unnoticed.
- No `./fbt lint_all`.
- Skipped in CI: the API-table and Sub-GHz golden checks (the checkout has no upstream/dev), and test_voice_build's resampler and model checks (build.yml installs no Python packages; requirements.txt lists numpy, scipy and kokoro-onnx).
- Pushes to the fork itself get no CI at all, so a fork commit that breaks the build is only seen when the pointer is bumped here.
- tools/flash_headroom.sh hard-codes the limit (880,640, SFSA 0xD7 for ble_light 1.20.0). The CI log says "Using guessed radio address 0x080D7000". Momentum's own CI reads the address from the copro manifest; a Momentum merge that brings a new radio stack would leave the hard-coded limit silently wrong.
- Fix: add the diff check and lint; fetch d3f89dfe2 shallowly (or pin the base in the tests); pip install numpy scipy in CI; derive the limit from lib/stm32wb_copro's manifest; and add a scheduled or manually started job that builds the fork's branch tip.

### R13. No way back for users (Medium)

- The only rollback instructions (device-notes, "Rolling back to official firmware") use Momentum-Firmware/scripts/selfupdate.py from the firmware tree. The README has none, and spec section 9 plans none.
- The updater deletes every file listed in the old /ext/Manifest before unpacking the new resources; the user's own files and the voice survive.
- A radio stack update when coming from other firmware adds reboots and minutes, so install.py's wait needs a generous time-out.
- Fix: install.py installs any update .tgz, the official one included, and can run `update restore`; a "Going back" section in the README.

### R14. The fork's inherited GitHub files (Medium-low, verified)

- The fork lists all five of Momentum's workflows as active but has no runs: only GitHub's default gate for forks holds them, and one click in the Actions tab opens it. Then build.yml would run on every push and every tag and fail its check of the API version against the official release channel on every sonar tag, webhook.yml would run on every push, issue and comment and fail for lack of secrets, and release.yml would download from an empty INDEXER_URL. No secrets exist, so nothing would leak; the harm is noise and red marks on release commits.
- FUNDING.yml shows a Sponsor button for Momentum's lead developer, CODEOWNERS lists the Momentum team, the bug template says "Flipper Zero unleashed firmware", and workflow_data/release.md has Next-Flip's download, Discord and donation links.
- Fix, without touching the files (they change upstream often, and every edit would conflict at merge time): in the fork's settings, disable Actions explicitly, turn off Issues, Discussions and Sponsorships, and set the description to point at flipper-access, where the README already sends crash reports.

### R15. API 87.3 and other people's apps (Low-medium, verified)

- The app loader checks only the API's major version; the minor check is commented out (lib/flipper_application/application_manifest.c lines 23 to 24 and 37 to 38). So the minor number does not decide whether an app loads.
- The fork removed the 90 JavaScript engine symbols under a minor bump. Catalog apps that import any of them fail to load with "missing imports". The spec's "apps from Momentum's catalog still load" needs that caveat, and apps built against a Sonar SDK that use the screen reader's exports will fail on other firmware.

### R16. Smaller ones (Low)

- Untagged builds report firmware.version "mntm-dev" (scripts/version.py line 44), and card_prune relies on that exact string. Changing it to something like "sonar-dev" would read better, but card_prune has to change in step.
- card_prune never prunes local builds from other branches (f7-update-mntm-<branch>-<hash>), each with a backup.tar.
- The action versions are real: the latest run, number 24, uses them and is green. For release.yml, which gets write access: pin actions by commit SHA, build without the toolchain cache, no cancel-in-progress, permissions per job.
- CHANGELOG.md, CONTRIBUTING.md and an issue template are absent. The template should ask for firmware.version and where the build came from (release, CI or local), since the elf that decodes a crash depends on it.

### A release checklist that closes these holes

1. Settings. On the fork: disable Actions; turn off Issues, Sponsorships and Discussions; a description that links to flipper-access. On both repositories: tag protection for sonar-*.
2. The voice pack and the install path: USTAR with folder entries and no PAX headers (R1); voice-*.tar ignored; full licence texts and the model's SHA-256 in the pack (R10); the install through `storage extract` (R4).
3. Documentation: the README's voice section and credits, a rollback section, CHANGELOG, CONTRIBUTING, the issue template, and a release-notes template with the Momentum base, the fork tag, the submodule commits, the known issues and the source offer.
4. release.yml on pushes of sonar-* tags, with pinned actions and no cache: check out at the tag, the submodule at depth 1, fetch the fork's tag explicitly, delete every other tag, assert that describe gives $TAG and HEAD is its commit; DIST_SUFFIX=$TAG and WORKFLOW_BRANCH_OR_TAG=screen-reader; the host tests with numpy and scipy, the build, the headroom against the radio address from the copro manifest, `git diff --exit-code`, and a check of version.json; the assets: update .tgz, elf and map, SDK zip, full source bundle, install zip, SHA256SUMS, and build attestations; a draft release, a prerelease for -rc.

## Tools and tests

Parity first, since everything else rests on it: the tools reviewer compiled the firmware's speech_text.c and speech_voice.c on the host and compared them with the generator for 12,271 words (the core strings, all 245 external apps, voice_words.txt, voice_names.txt and every letter clip). The generator's bucket and file name match the firmware's speech_voice_path for every word, the generator never makes a clip the device would not ask for, and all 36 letter and digit clips are in voice_words.txt. The words the device asks for that can never be made (203 from the core strings, 3,349 with all apps) are all longer than 16 letters or mix letters and digits, as "Limits of the voice" says.

### T1. Interrupting a sync can hang the tool and leave the device muted (High, verified in the code)

- Where: tools/voice_build.py lines 655 to 716 (sync) and 626 to 652 (pull_missing); Momentum-Firmware/scripts/flipper/storage.py lines 67 to 89 (BufferedRead.until loops with no deadline, over 2-second reads) and 233 to 270 (send_file: `storage write_chunk`, then "Ready", then the data, then the prompt).
- What happens: a Ctrl-C between the write_chunk command and its data leaves the device waiting for file bytes. The finally block then sends "sr voice on", which the device takes as 12 bytes of clip data, and waits forever for a prompt that never comes. A second Ctrl-C raises KeyboardInterrupt, which `except Exception` does not catch, so the restore is abandoned and storage.stop() is skipped. The device stays muted until a reboot or `sr voice on`. The README mentions this for a cable pulled or a process killed, not for a plain Ctrl-C.
- Fix: catch BaseException around the restore; give the reads a deadline; and, better, let the firmware lift the mute itself when the console session closes or after a time-out, so that no host failure can leave a blind user without speech.

### T2. The tools that talk to the device need Python 3.10, and the README does not say so (Medium, verified)

- scripts/flipper/storage.py line 50 annotates `s: str | bytes` without `from __future__ import annotations`, so importing it raises TypeError on Python 3.9, the python3 that comes with the Mac's command line tools. `--sync`, `--pull-missing` and `--card-apps` then crash, and so will install.py if it reuses the module.
- Fix: say Python 3.10 or newer in the README and check the version at start-up; the spec already requires 3.10 for install.py.

### T3. strip_js_symbols.py can bump the API version twice (Medium, verified)

- Where: tools/strip_js_symbols.py lines 63 to 82 (FORK_MINOR_STEP, 2, line 36); docs/device-notes.md line 197, which says to run it in any case.
- What happens: the script adds 2 to the table's current version whenever it removes a JavaScript row or adds one of the fork's rows. After a clean merge that brings one new mjs_ row, the fork's own table goes from 87.3 to 87.5. Nothing tests it: CI skips the golden check, and the idempotence test has no JavaScript rows.
- Fix: compute the version as upstream's own plus the step, reading upstream's table (git show MERGE_HEAD:targets/f7/api_symbols.csv), instead of adding the step to whatever is there.

### T4. Too much output for a screen reader (Medium, verified)

- storage.py's send_file prints a progress line for every clip, about 10,800 lines for a full sync, and once the port dies, voice_build.py (lines 696 to 699) logs "sync: failed" once for every remaining word, then a traceback.
- Fix: stop the loop on serial.SerialException or OSError; run send_file under contextlib.redirect_stdout; keep the loop's own line every hundred clips.

### T5. synced.json is not tied to a card (Medium-low, verified with a fake storage)

- With a complete synced.json and a fresh card (wiped, replaced, or a second Flipper), a sync sends nothing, then fails with an unhandled "Storage error: ... /ext/sr/voices/heart/voice.txt: file/dir not exist". With the set's folder present but incomplete, the missing clips are skipped silently. The README says to use --sync-all after a wipe; the failure mode is still a traceback.
- Fix: write an identifier to the card with the set (in voice.txt or a .sync-id file) and trust synced.json only when it matches; or fall back to --sync-all when voice.txt is absent.

### T6. --pull-missing removes the card's list before anything is made from it (Medium-low, verified)

- voice_build.py lines 638 to 644 remove missing.txt right after reading it; the words live only in memory. A build or sync that fails or is interrupted (a Kokoro run takes about 17 minutes) loses them until each word is spoken again after a reboot.
- Fix: keep the pulled words in a file under the set's folder and remove the card's list only after a successful sync.

### T7. How a clip with an apostrophe sounds depends on which apps were extracted (Medium-low, verified with the reviewer's harness)

- With all apps extracted there are 18 collisions between words that differ only by an apostrophe. Three are audibly wrong: "were" is recorded as "we're", "ill" as "i'll" and "id" as "i'd". A run with a different scope picks a different spoken form, changes the clip's hash, and remakes and resends it.
- Fix: when a word also occurs without an apostrophe, keep the plain form (unless voice_names.txt says otherwise), and log the collision.

### T8. The port is found only on a Mac, and a busy port hangs silently (Low-medium)

- voice_build.py line 838 and the console tools look only for /dev/cu.usbmodemflip_*. Elsewhere, without --port, pyserial fails with "Port must be configured" (the README does document --port). The port is not opened exclusively and the start-up read has no deadline, so when qFlipper or flipper_log.py already holds the port, the tool waits forever without a word.
- Fix: find the port by USB id 0483:5740 with serial.tools.list_ports, as spec section 6 plans for install.py; open it exclusively; put a deadline on the start-up read.

### T9. App names are not extracted (Low-medium, verified with the reviewer's harness)

- The Apps menu shows each app's name from its application.fam, but the extraction reads only .c and .h files (voice_build.py lines 251 to 270). Of 456 words in app names, 222 are not made by --extract and 35 not even by --extract-all.
- Fix: add the manifests' name and fap_category strings as a word source.

### T10. Smaller ones (Low)

- upstream_check.sh exits 128 with git's own error when the upstream remote is missing (lines 42 to 48), though its header promises 1; the merge routine never says to add the remote.
- A clip that comes out all zeros is written as 30 ms of silence and counted as made (voice_build.py lines 529 to 536), so the device plays silence instead of spelling the word; only --check validates clips.
- A write error after the data (a full card) is never read, so the clip is recorded as synced and the leftover bytes reach the console as input (storage.py lines 259 to 261).
- --jobs 0 fails with ValueError, or ZeroDivisionError for Kokoro (voice_build.py lines 547 to 553).
- The expander is built only with clang (line 247): a Linux machine with only gcc, or Windows, gets a FileNotFoundError traceback.
- synced.json is saved only at every hundredth word and never on Ctrl-C, so an interrupted sync resends work (time, not data).

### T11. What the tests do not cover

- sync(), pull_missing() and the voice guard; Python's FNV-1a against the C one; the 16-letter clip limit against the 20-character pieces of speech_text.c. The reviewer's parity harness runs in a quarter of a second and could become a check.
- Each new clip costs two round trips, a `storage stat` and the write. Listing each of the 64 folders once and skipping the stat for absent files should save about one round trip per clip (an estimate, not measured).

## Memory and speed

Sizes computed on the struct layouts for the Cortex-M4 (the speech engine's measured with arm-none-eabi-gcc). The reader's own heap today: the service structure about 9.6 KB, the speech engine 10,904 bytes, a 512-byte file buffer, two 3 KB thread stacks: about 27 KB, which matches the roadmap's figure. The flash headroom is 14,344 bytes.

Heap:

- The `working` frame is redundant: sr_model_ready copies ready into working and models it while holding the mutex anyway. Model the ready frame in place and keep it as the last frame for `sr screen`: 1,988 bytes.
- SR_MAX_ANNOUNCEMENTS is 4, but sr_model_process emits at most one announcement per frame: three unused 168-byte slots, 504 bytes.
- A per-frame string pool of about 640 bytes with 14-byte records, instead of 32 records of 48 characters each, would save about 900 bytes per frame buffer, 1.8 to 2.7 KB in all (an estimate).
- The speech ring holds 8,192 duty bytes of 20 µs slots, rendered through a callback per slot. TIM16 has a repetition counter: with an auto-reload of 199 (200 duty steps), a prescaler of 4 and a repetition count of 3, the carrier is 64 kHz and the DMA request comes at exactly 16 kHz, one per clip sample. The ring would hold samples, 2,624 bytes for the same two halves of 82 ms (5.5 KB less), with no resampling and a third of the per-sample work. It needs a listening test on the piezo (a 64 kHz carrier, 200 duty steps instead of 256), and `sr play`'s other sample rates would need their own timer settings, or go.
- The speech worker's deepest chains come to about 0.6 to 0.9 KB, from the frame sizes in the objects of this commit: its 3 KB stack could be 2 KB, 1 KB of heap. Confirm with `sr status`'s stack watermark.
- The expansion buffer `expanded` (800 bytes) could go with an incremental expander, about 600 bytes, which also removes S2 at its source.
- The missing-word state is 528 bytes; 16-bit hashes and a packed log would save about 250.
- The `sr` command's stack, 4 KB to 2 KB (F4).

Flash:

- tanhf and expm1f take 872 bytes, linked only for the volume curve in speech_pcm.c line 15; a 256-byte constant curve with integer volume scaling saves 600 to 850 bytes.
- The stop word table is an array of pointers, about 430 bytes; one string with the words separated by NULs saves about 400.
- `sr` as a CLI plugin on the card, as `input` already is: an estimated 4 to 6 KB, against about 20 more exports and needing the card for `sr` (only the chord is needed for recovery).

Time:

- Skip identical frames: the main menu and every variable item list redraw every 333 ms whatever happens, and each frame costs a 1,988-byte copy under the mutex and a thread wake-up, then a model run that finds nothing. An identical frame can never produce an announcement, so sr_frame_end can compare with the frame last handed over and skip it: never while turned_on is set, and invalidated wherever have_prev is reset and after a dropped frame. Little CPU, but fewer wake-ups and fewer windows for F6.
- Swap the model's prev and current buffers instead of copying 1,352 bytes on every model run.
- Pauses and letter gaps could fill the ring with silence directly instead of through the per-slot callback (a 300 ms pause is 15,000 calls), and at volume 0 an item could complete muted without opening a clip.
- Replace the per-item storage_sd_status round trip with a flag kept from the storage pubsub, which also fixes the card swap of S5.
- Skip the clip lookup for words the generator can never make (longer than 16 letters, or letters mixed with digits): each costs a whole-folder scan today.
- Clip lookups read a folder's entries one by one, about 0.17 ms each on this card. With about 10,800 clips in 64 folders, a folder holds about 170, and the longest open measured is 66 ms against a half-buffer of 82 ms, so a larger vocabulary may start to underrun. Two levels of folders (16 by 16, about 42 clips in each) would cut the entries read per lookup to about a third, at the cost of a layout change in the firmware and the generator together.
- For users, the voice pack's size (R11) is the biggest lever: the install time is dominated by 100 MB over the console.

## Checked and found sound

- The input filter with the device's own keys: Press and Release pairing, and the order of the timer thread's Long and Repeat against the input thread's Release (furi_timer_stop returns only after the timer thread has processed the stop). Remote control and `sr watch` publish around the filter and never touch the chord state.
- The change throttle: no busy loop when the change delay changes between reads, tick wrap handled, values always within 500 to 10,000.
- Concurrent settings saves serialise (storage_file_open waits while the file is open), and LittleFS commits on close, so a reset mid-save keeps the old file. The last byte of sr_voice_set is always a terminator.
- The reader thread's deepest path is about 1.3 KB of its 3 KB stack (reasoned, not measured).
- The crash handler's saved register at index 12 is the link register; the build id is read from flash with a range check, without allocation or locks; the crash popup's reads are bounded.
- The speech engine: speech_hw_start and speech_hw_stop pair up on every path, superseded items and time-outs included, and the interrupt is always unregistered; stale half flags are cleared before each start, a stale stop is judged by the generation, and no wake-up is lost; stats.speaking is cleared on all six exit paths; the speaker is released only when idle, after the hardware stopped; the only lock order is the reader's mutex before the speech mutex, and the worker never does I/O while holding the speech mutex.
- Fuzzing under the address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers: 300,000 inputs through speech_text_expand, speech_text_spell_copy and speech_voice_next_word, and 200,000 texts through speech_queue_push_parts, found no out-of-bounds access and no undefined behaviour; the expansion is always terminated, printable and under 800 characters; parts are at most 159 characters. Clip paths are at most 86 bytes (the buffer is 96).
- The GUI tap's hint lifetimes: every hint the fork sets reaches its intended string, and canvas_tap_set_layer clears leftovers at the start of the frame and before every view port. The shortened-text hint's prefix check protects elements_string_fit_width. Inversion detection is right in dark mode, XOR never counts as inverted, and the tap is removed during direct draw and restored after. No core screen came near 32 records or 16 rows.
- With animations off, every animation manager entry point is guarded, and the dolphin's first-in-first-out queue prevents a double level up. The extra dolphin publish is harmless. The new settings fields are appended at the end of the structure, so apps built against the old layout still work.
- The PIN lock disables the USB console unless allow_locked_rpc_usb is set, so the `sr` commands are no way around it. Two `sr watch` sessions cannot cause a use-after-free.
- The fork's own files compile without a warning under GCC 13 with the firmware's flags (compile only; see below).
- The host tests pass: 195 screen model, 968 speech text, 156 queue, 597 PCM, 4,573 voice, 208 chord and 119 throttle checks, and every Python check (the resampler and Kokoro model checks were skipped for want of numpy, scipy and kokoro-onnx).
- The generator and the firmware agree on every clip path (see "Tools and tests").

### Checks on the device

These need Robin's device, or a session on the Mac with the Flipper attached.
